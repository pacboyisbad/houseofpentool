Three kitchens.Three needs.One mayo range.

A redesign of Funtrip's 1 kg food-service mayonnaise. Three packs built around the three businesses that buy them, not ranked from best to cheapest.

01 · Where we started

Three packs. Three different brands.

Funtrip sells three eggless mayonnaises to kitchens across India. Each pack had been designed on its own, at a different time, in a different style. Nothing on the shelf said they were a family, and nothing said who each one was for.

Eggless MayonnaisePurple, script type, burger in a milk splash
Veg MayoFlat yellow, condensed type, bowl and lettuce
Mayonnaise LitePale green, script type, heart and bowl
No system

Three layouts, three type styles

The logo moved, the product name changed form on every pack and the colours had no relationship.

Hidden names

Variant names hard to find

"Lite" sat in a small red ribbon. "Mayonnaise" and "Mayo" were used for the same line.

Generic food

Bowls and lettuce said nothing

The imagery showed mayo, not the kitchen it was made for, so buyers had no reason to pick one.

Mixed claims

"Pure", "Egg less", "Eggless"

The one claim every buyer cares about was written three different ways.

02 · The insight

Positioning is deciding who should feel what.

A restaurant, a sandwich shop and a momo cart all buy a 1 kg pouch of mayo, but for different reasons. So the range is designed around the kitchen it serves.

The easy answer

Good. Better. Best.Nope

Premium · bestVeg · middleLite · cheapest

A quality ladder makes Lite the one you settle for, and makes Veg look like a compromise.

Our answer

Three businesses. Three jobs.

RestaurantQSR / CaféStreet food

Each variant is the right choice for its kitchen. Nobody is trading down.

03 · Range architecture

Burger → Sandwich → Momos.

The food on each pack tells you which kitchen it belongs to. Left to right, the range moves from the restaurant plate to the café counter to the street cart.

04 · The pack system

One layout. Four things change.

Every pack is built on the same grid. The brand, the structure and the pour stay fixed, so the family reads as one. Only the colours, the name and the food change, so each variant is easy to tell apart.

The logo never moves. It sits on the seam between the outer field and the window, so the brand is read first and the variant second.

05 · Colour pairs

One colour for the shelf. One for the story.

The outer field is loud, so the variant can be found across a store room. The window colour is the variant's voice: deep slate for the restaurant, open sky blue for the café, leaf green for the street.
